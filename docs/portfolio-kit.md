## LinkedIn launch draft

I built a healthcare product analytics case study focused on a deceptively simple question: patient portals provide access to information, but do they help patients finish the work around care?

Using a MyChart-style portal as the context, I examined scheduling, outside records, visit documentation, preparation, and follow-up. The research changed my original framing. Many capabilities already exist; the more defensible opportunity is making them consistently available, understandable, and connected across the care journey.

The resulting concept, Care Timeline, shows what happened, what is pending, who owns the next step, and which source is authoritative. I also designed the MVP backlog, architecture, KPI tree, experiment plan, and responsible AI controls.

The AI layer is intentionally narrow: it can explain approved content in plain language, organize documented tasks, and help draft questions. It cannot diagnose, triage, recommend treatment, or change the medical record.

This project reflects the kind of work I want to keep doing at the intersection of healthcare, product analytics, patient experience, and responsible AI.

## Interview answer

I started with five friction points from lived experience, then tested whether they generalized. The most important decision was changing the thesis when the evidence showed that modern portals already support many of the individual capabilities. Instead of presenting another feature redesign, I focused on a measurable workflow problem: patients may have access to information without a clear understanding of what is complete, what is pending, and what action comes next.

I designed a deterministic Care Timeline first, because I wanted to test whether better orchestration alone improves outcomes. Then I added an optional, source-linked AI explanation layer with strict non-goals. The north-star metric is closed-loop completion, balanced by clinical-safety, equity, privacy, and staff-workload guardrails. That process demonstrates how I use evidence to revise a product thesis rather than defend the first idea.
